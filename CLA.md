in the contributions you make, so the project can offer HAL under both the
GNU Affero General Public Licence v3.0 and a commercial licence. It protects
you as much as the project: it records that you had the right to contribute
what you contributed.

### Before you use this

Placeholders to fill: **`<PROJECT-OWNER-LEGAL-NAME>`**, **`<JURISDICTION>`**.

This draft follows the structure of the widely-used Apache Individual CLA, with
section 2 extended to grant the relicensing and sublicensing rights that Apache's
own CLA does not provide — that extension is exactly what dual licensing
requires, and exactly the part most worth having reviewed.

**Have a solicitor with software-licensing experience read this before the
repository goes public.** Whether section 2 actually conveys the rights you need
is jurisdiction-dependent, and the failure mode is invisible until you have
already sold a commercial licence you cannot honour. It is a cheap review and an
expensive mistake.

Also consider whether you need a **corporate CLA** as well. This one binds an
individual; a contributor whose employer owns their output needs their employer
to sign, and organisations contributing at any scale will expect a corporate
form to exist.
